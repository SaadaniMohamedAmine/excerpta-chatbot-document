Based on the 1927 Solvay Conference transcripts, the discussion on wave-particle duality centered primarily around the interpretations presented by Bohr, Einstein, and de Broglie.

"The debate highlighted a fundamental epistemological divide. De Broglie presented his pilot-wave theory, attempting to maintain deterministic trajectories. In contrast, Bohr argued for complementarity, suggesting that wave and particle descriptions are mutually exclusive but jointly necessary to describe quantum phenomena completely."

description Solvay_1927_Transcripts.pdf, p. 114

Einstein, famously, expressed deep dissatisfaction with the statistical nature of the emerging Copenhagen interpretation, leading to his thought experiments designed to demonstrate its incompleteness. The consensus largely shifted towards Bohr's complementary view, solidifying the Copenhagen interpretation's dominance for decades.
